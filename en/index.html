Experience is in the details.

A written procedure describes the steps. First-person video shows how they are carried out: the hands, the tools and the adjustments each task requires.

03 · What counts

Only accepted hours count.

A recorded hour is not necessarily an accepted hour. Acceptance depends on the program’s review criteria.

The example distinguishes tasks from breaks; it does not replace acceptance criteria.

The proposed model calculates payment from accepted hours, not shift length.

04 · The money

One accepted hour, three shares

Labs pay us by the accepted hour. We split each accepted hour three ways: your business, your workers and us. It is extra income for work your team already does.

The pay rule

If your workers get nothing, the program does not work.

  1. Workers get more
  2. They record more hours
  3. Everyone earns more

It is a loop: the more your workers get, the more hours they record, and the more everyone earns. Then it starts again.

05 · For your team

“Are we training the robots that replace us?”

It is the first question your workers will ask you. It is fair, and it deserves a straight answer.

We do not make promises about the future of jobs. This is what is true today:

  • Joining is voluntary. Workers who want it go first.

  • Every accepted hour is paid. We split it three ways, and one share goes to the workers.

  • The explanation comes before the camera. You and our team explain the program before anyone records.

  • Anyone can stop recording at any time.

  • The video trains robots. It is never used to rate or monitor anyone’s work.

Consent rules in our first four countries

Each country sets its own consent rules for personal data. In short:

  • Panama

    The data regulator (ANTAI) asks for prior, informed consent that can be proven.

    Source: ANTAI guide (PDF, in Spanish) (opens in a new tab)

  • Colombia

    No activity can be conditioned on giving sensitive data.

    Source: Decreto 1377 de 2013, art. 6 (in Spanish) (opens in a new tab)

  • Peru

    Consent must be free, prior, express, unambiguous and informed.

    Source: DS 016-2024-JUS (PDF, in Spanish) (opens in a new tab)

  • Mexico

    Sensitive data needs express, written consent.

    Source: LFPDPPP 2025, arts. 7 and 8 (in Spanish) (opens in a new tab)

08 · Before you decide

Common questions

01What does my business have to do?

Your team keeps doing its usual work. Workers who join wear a camera while they work. You set what your workers get for each accepted hour, and you host us on setup day. We bring the cameras, train your staff and sell the video to robotics labs.

02What does it cost me?

Nothing. There is no cost to your business, and we bring the cameras at no cost.

03How much does my business get?

Labs pay for every accepted hour, and that payment is split three ways: your business, your workers and us.

04Who decides what my workers get?

You do. We guide; we do not force. If workers get nothing, the program does not work: the more they get, the more hours they record and the more everyone earns.

We pay workers directly.

05Do my workers have to join?

No. Joining is voluntary, and workers who want it go first. You and our team explain the program before anyone records.

An hour of video of someone actually working, with their hands in frame. Breaks do not count.
