01 · Experience

Experience is in the details.

A written procedure describes the steps. First-person video shows how they are carried out: the hands, the tools and the adjustments each task requires.

It is also called egocentric video. It is used to study physical tasks and to help train robotics models.

Packing orders, handling materials, using tools: your team knows every detail of the work. That know-how is what we record.

03 · What counts

Only accepted hours count.

A recorded hour is not always an accepted hour. Only real work with hands in view can count; breaks never do.

The example distinguishes tasks from breaks; it does not replace acceptance criteria.

Payment is calculated from accepted hours, not shift length.

04 · The money

One accepted hour, three shares

Every accepted hour earns a payment that is split three ways: your business, your team and Mirentor. It is extra income for work your team already does.

The pay rule

If your team earns nothing, the program does not work.

  1. Your team earns more
  2. Records more hours
  3. Everyone earns more

It is a loop: the more your team earns, the more hours it records, and the more everyone earns. Then it starts again.

05 · For your team

“Are we training the robots that replace us?”

It is the first question your workers will ask you. It is fair, and it deserves a straight answer.

We do not make promises about the future of jobs. This is what is true today:

  • Joining is voluntary. Workers who want it go first.

  • Every accepted hour is paid. We split it three ways, and one share goes to the workers.

  • The explanation comes before the camera. You and our team explain the program before anyone records.

  • Anyone can stop recording at any time.

  • The video helps train robotics models. It is never used to rate or monitor anyone’s work.

Consent rules in our first four countries

Each country sets its own consent rules for personal data. In short:

  • Panama

    The data regulator (ANTAI) asks for prior, informed consent that can be proven.

    Source: ANTAI guide (PDF, in Spanish) (opens in a new tab)

  • Colombia

    No activity can be conditioned on giving sensitive data.

    Source: Decreto 1377 de 2013, art. 6 (in Spanish) (opens in a new tab)

  • Peru

    Consent must be free, prior, express, unambiguous and informed.

    Source: DS 016-2024-JUS (PDF, in Spanish) (opens in a new tab)

  • Mexico

    Sensitive data needs express, written consent.

    Source: LFPDPPP 2025, arts. 7 and 8 (in Spanish) (opens in a new tab)

08 · Before you decide

Common questions

01What does my business have to do?

Your team keeps doing its usual work. People who join wear a camera while they work: on at the start of the shift, off before the restroom, charged overnight. Your site needs Wi-Fi and a project manager who owns the program. You set what your team gets for each accepted hour, and you host us on setup day. We bring the cameras, train your staff and collect the data.

02What does it cost me?

Nobody pays to join. We pay for the cameras up front, their cost comes out of your business's earnings over time, and in the end the cameras are yours.

03How much does my business get?

Every accepted hour earns a payment, split three ways: your business, your team and Mirentor.

Your share per hour $

See the split

04Who decides what my workers get?

You do, and we help you set it. If workers get nothing, the program does not work: the more they get, the more hours they record and the more everyone earns.

We pay your business, and your workers get their share through your usual payroll.

05Do my workers have to join?

No. Joining is voluntary, and workers who want it go first. You and our team explain the program before anyone records.

Rules by country

06What counts as an accepted hour?

An hour of video of someone actually working, with their hands in frame. Breaks never count.

See what counts

07Which cities come first?

Panama City comes first. Medellín, Lima and Mexico City are also on our first list. We open one city at a time and choose the next by how many businesses are ready there. US businesses can join too, but Latin America comes first.
